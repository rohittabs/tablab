What it is

A tab editor made for learning

Write down what you are learning, hear it played back, then practice it slowly until it sits under your fingers. TabLab keeps the screen simple, so the music stays in front.

Getting started

Your first tab in a minute

  1. Open TabLab. It starts with an example song: press Space to hear it.
  2. Click New and choose guitar or ukulele, the tuning, the tempo and the time signature.
  3. Click a string (or use the arrow keys) and type a fret number.
  4. Press → for the next beat. At the end of a bar, it adds one.
  5. Press Space to play it back from the cursor.

Playback

Hear it, and follow along

Space plays from the cursor with a plucked string sound. The playhead follows the music and the page scrolls along with it. Esc stops.

The volume menu has separate sliders for the tab sound, the metronome and the backing track.

Loop

Repeat only the hard bars

  1. Press L to loop the bar you are on.
  2. Or select some beats first (Shift and the arrows), then press L to loop just those.
  3. Start slow, then speed it up a little each time round.

Backing tracks

Play along with the real recording

  1. Click Backing track, then Load audio file. Or drop the file on the page.
  2. Press Auto sync. TabLab finds the tempo and where bar 1 starts.
  3. Press Space to check. If it feels twice too fast or too slow, use ÷2 or ×2.

MP3, M4A, WAV, OGG and the other audio files your browser can play. Switch off Tab sound to hear just the recording.

Syncing by hand

Tap it, drag it, nudge it

  • Tap tempo: tap along with the beat to set the tempo.
  • Drag the 1 line in the waveform onto the first beat of the song.
  • Or press Bar 1 starts now while the track plays, right on that first beat.
  • Nudge bar 1 earlier or later in 10 ms steps.
  • Zoom into the waveform to see the beats clearly.

Real bands drift

Pin any bar to its beat

If the band speeds up or slows down, drag that bar's line onto its beat. The bar is pinned there, and the bars in between stretch to follow. Double click a pinned bar to unpin it.

In TabLab's tests in Chrome, the recording and the tab stay within about 3 ms of each other at full speed, and about 15 ms when slowed down or sped up. The recording keeps its pitch.

Saving

It saves as you go

  • Every change is kept in this browser straight away.
  • Open lists the songs you saved and three example songs.
  • Save (Ctrl S) downloads a .tablab file. Open it again later, or drop it on the page.
  • A backing track is kept with its song in the browser, so it is there next time.

Privacy

There is no server

TabLab is a set of files that runs in your browser. Your songs and audio never leave your device.

  • No account to make
  • Nothing is uploaded
  • No ads and no tracking
  • Open source: the code is on GitHub

Good to know

A few honest details

  • Songs live in this browser. Clearing your browser data deletes them, so save a .tablab file of anything you care about.
  • A .tablab file holds the song and its sync settings, not the audio. Send the audio file along with it.
  • TabLab has its own simple file format. It does not open files from other tab programs.
  • Keeping the pitch of a slowed down recording needs Chrome 86, Firefox 101, Safari 17.2 or newer.
  • Auto sync works best on music with a steady beat, like drums or strumming.
